# Companion greetings, conversations and jukebox dancing

Owner-requested addition on `peepo-companion`, based on `7f9b6ea1`. Implementation: OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), under the owner's existing tool/model exception.

## Player behavior

- An idle Peepo or Jughead waves with its free left hand when its owner comes within six blocks with line of sight. The wave lasts up to two seconds. The owner must leave the eight-block vicinity before a later arrival can greet again, with a minimum 60-second cooldown. Arrival checks happen every four to five seconds when idle, so a busy companion can greet after finishing its activity.
- Two standing, idle companions within three blocks can face each other for a six-second conversation. They alternate small arm gestures, listening nods and quiet, pitched vanilla frog sounds every 1.5 seconds. Each companion waits 90–120 seconds before another conversation. These are gestures and sounds, without chat messages or speech bubbles.
- A completed conversation now chooses one ending: a two-second high-five, a 2.6-second shared laugh, or a 1.2-second farewell wave with a responding nod. The listener starts laughing slightly before the speaker. High-fives include a palm tap and a small pleased nod. Normal work, hunger, recovery, commands and settings preempt these endings immediately; interruptions do not add a farewell delay.
- High-fives need both companions' hands empty and nearly level ground. Their native short arms meet at 0.46–0.54 blocks between centers, with 0.05 blocks added per pumpkin costume for clearance. Other distances use a farewell instead. Conversations can start from 0.44 blocks apart; neither companion navigates toward the other. Moving, changing equipment or losing a partner cancels an active high-five.
- Both variants and the pumpkin costume use the existing rig. Held tools and raised lights keep their right-hand pose; social gestures use the free hand when carrying an item.
- Shift-right-click, open **Routine**, and toggle **Social: On/Off**. On is the default, including existing saves. Only the owner or an authorized party member can change this through the existing validated menu.
- Greetings may happen while standing still in Follow or Stay. Conversations are disabled in those two command modes. Neither behavior walks to a partner, joins a seated/sleeping pose, or interrupts productive work, transport, food, recovery or settings editing. Pending delivery cargo also prevents socializing. Available higher-priority work can preempt a conversation.
- A loaded vanilla jukebox actively playing within eight blocks can start an idle dance through the same **Social** toggle. Both variants share a two-second groove with alternating foot taps, a small body sway, loose arm swings and a head bob; each eight-second phrase may add a free-hand wave or wiggle. Entry includes a small head tilt and eased movement. They dance where they already stand and never navigate toward music. Stay allows dancing at the commanded spot; Follow allows it while already near the owner, and following movement immediately takes precedence. Jobs, cargo, eating, recovery, rest, commands and menu editing also interrupt immediately. A natural song end, removal, unload or loss of range uses a half-second settle. A record left inserted after its song ends is not a dance trigger.

## Server behavior and compatibility

The server chooses partners and animation phases. Conversations use a vanilla goal below work/rest/transport/commands and above wandering. One pair leader chooses the ending once and starts both clips on the same game tick. Complementary pose IDs and a single local-space palm target use entity metadata; there are no per-frame animation packets. Both variants, mixed pairs and costumes share the clips. Pair cancellation releases both partners on commands, menus, unload, loss of eligibility, separation or timeout. Active references and poses are never saved; reload resumes normal behavior. Remaining greeting/conversation cooldowns and the per-companion toggle are additive save fields. Rest departures, carried-item gestures and work reactions cannot overlap an idle social encounter.

Discovery uses a separate server-wide FIFO budget of one social scan per tick across all dimensions; it does not consume existing work-search or navigation allowances. Searches are staggered 80–99 ticks and query only the local loaded AABB, with at most 16 companion results before eligibility filtering. A detected nearby conversation prevents another pair from starting. No social navigation, chunk tickets, global entity scan or offline simulation is introduced. Active pairs validate their partner and line of sight each tick; large-population performance still needs measurement. `/peepobudget` includes admitted/deferred social searches.

Pose changes, their start time and the ending's palm target are synchronized through entity data; animation runs client-side on the existing model. The original menu appends one data field and statuses append Greeting/Chatting, preserving previous indices and inventory slots. The new endings use the existing Social toggle. Update both server and clients together.

This is cosmetic companion behavior at the existing companion-management tier. It has no recipe, resource input/output, extra energy cost, reward, buff, unlock or dependency. Existing energy recovery continues unchanged. Food, machinery and progression remain independent of social participation.

### Jukebox discovery and animation

`CompanionMusic` maintains one shared index per server dimension, partitioned by chunk and 16-block vertical section. Native jukebox song-change/removal hooks register or invalidate sources, with a single first-tick check restoring saved playback. Chunk unload and server stop clear entries. Entries hold weak block-entity references; discovery never reads surrounding blocks, loads chunks or scans companions. An eight-block query touches at most eight section buckets and inspects at most 64 sources. Extreme concentrations beyond that candidate cap may omit a nearby source; the bound is intentional. Music discovery shares the existing single admitted social scan per server tick, staggered at four to five seconds per idle companion. Active dancing only validates its selected source, eligibility and range, without repeating discovery or requesting navigation.

The selected source remains stable while dancing, even when another jukebox starts nearby. Native playback progress and song duration determine the shared phase and expiry; the song's `JUKEBOX_PLAYABLE` component supports compatible custom discs without a hard-coded list. It is a fixed dance rhythm synchronized to playback progress, not audio analysis or per-track BPM matching. Only vanilla jukebox playback is integrated; note blocks and Jugcraft music blocks are not triggers. Two additive entity-data clocks distinguish entry from song progress and natural exit, while appending the Dancing status preserves earlier status indices. There are no per-frame animation messages, and no dance state is saved. Update server and clients together.

The clip uses the existing companion rig with native limb lengths, attached hips and a small weight shift. Costume parts follow the body; the right arm preserves its held-item pose, including upright torches, while the free arm dances. It does not change energy recovery, inventory, drops or sounds.

The shared owner library and full catalog were inspected for compatible frog sounds and greeting/conversation clips, then checked again for high-five, laughter and farewell assets. No compatible companion clips were found. Procedural poses extend the existing authored companion rig; no imported animation files or new rendering framework are used. Sounds reference Minecraft's built-in `FROG_AMBIENT` and `FROG_STEP` events without copying Mojang files. Shared laughter adds at most two quiet sounds per pair, and a high-five adds one soft contact sound. Existing companion textures/models are unchanged. Feet remain planted, arms retain their native length, and held lights retain their right-hand pose during free-hand laughter/farewells. Vanilla goals and the current inventory-style menu are extended under the framework catalog's existing-feature allowances. No new assets or dependencies are bundled.

The owner library catalog was also searched for dance/dancing clips before implementing jukebox dancing. No compatible companion clip was found. The original procedural dance extends the same rig, without new textures, sounds, imported assets or dependencies.
