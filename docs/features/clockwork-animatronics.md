# Clockwork Animatronics

Status: all six parts implemented: 1 (the Organ-Grinder Skeleton), 2 (the Animatronic Werewolf), 3 (the Flying Crank Ghost), 4 (the Clockwork Raven), 5 (the Pumpkin-Headed Drummer) and 6 (the Show Controller). CI builds them and runs their game tests and client screenshots; not yet played by hand.
Proposal issue: none. The owner asked for it directly on 28 October 2026, as set 9 of the ten sets of fall and Halloween decor ("Then I want you to do 1-10 in parts in that order one by one"): an organ-grinder skeleton with a hurdy-gurdy, an animatronic werewolf on a turntable, a ghost on a crank wire, a mechanical raven and a pumpkin-headed drummer, on the power network, brass and steam at first and a dieselpunk steel version later.
Owner: @jimbozoomer-byte
Target milestone and tier: the bronze age of kinetic power ([kinetic-power.md](kinetic-power.md)). The figures are made from vanilla things and a clock; they move on kinetic energy from a hand crank or a steam engine, through iron shafts and brass gearboxes, or from the JE network through an electric motor.
Primary specialty and supported player role: building, with engineering. A haunt's builder sets the figures up; an engineer runs the power to them. Nothing in it is needed to progress.

## Player experience
Halloween figures that move by clockwork. Each runs on kinetic power: a shaft, gearbox or source touching its lower block gives it what its clockwork needs. It runs only on all of that. Its power stopped, it runs down over half a second and comes to rest where it is.

### Part 1: the Organ-Grinder Skeleton
- **Organ-Grinder Skeleton**: a skeleton 1.75 m tall in a battered black top hat and a moth-eaten red waistcoat, standing behind a street barrel organ (a hurdy-gurdy, as an organ-grinder's is called) on a two-wheeled cart. Two blocks tall.
  - The organ is a mahogany case 47 cm wide, bound in brass, its front a row of gilded pipes on red cloth, with a crank on its right side. The cart has spoked wheels 37 cm across, a prop under its front and two handles.
  - The skeleton's left arm hangs at its side, a tin cup for coins on a finger.
  - **It stands facing whoever places it**, and needs the block above free.
  - **It runs on 4 KE a tick**, taken from a shaft, gearbox or kinetic source touching its lower block: a hand crank turned beside it, a steam engine's shaft line, or an electric motor.
  - **While it runs**:
    - its right hand turns the organ's crank, once round a second, the arm bending at the elbow to follow the handle;
    - the organ plays its waltz, an original tune in A minor, eight bars long, the melody on the flute and the bass on the first beat of each bar;
    - its head nods once a bar, and its jaw drops on each note of the melody.
  - **Its power stopped**, it keeps going for half a second, then the crank runs down and stops where it is, its head and jaw settle, and the organ falls silent. Started again, the organ plays from the top.
  - Recipe: black wool for the hat over a note block between two bone blocks, over a clock between two planks.
  - Broken, either half brings the whole figure down, and it drops once.

### Part 2: the Animatronic Werewolf
- **Animatronic Werewolf**: a werewolf 1.75 m tall hunched (taller upright), in torn denim trousers, standing on a riveted brass turntable 69 cm across on a black iron plinth with a brass gear housing at its front. Two blocks tall.
  - It has digitigrade legs, with long clawed paws, its hocks high behind and its shins forward, and long clawed arms. Its head has amber eyes, a black nose, fangs and two pointed ears.
  - **It runs on 8 KE a tick**, as the skeleton does on 4, from a shaft, gearbox or kinetic source touching its lower block.
  - **While it runs**:
    - the turntable turns it slowly round, once every 16 seconds;
    - every 8 seconds it howls. Its arms swing up from its sides over 0.6 seconds and its head goes back, its jaw dropping. It holds that for 1.2 seconds, howling a wolf's howl, deep and slow, then lowers them again.
  - **Its power stopped**, the turntable runs down over a second and stops where it is, and it lowers its arms. Started again, its first howl comes 5.6 seconds in.
  - Recipe: bones and brown wool round a clock, over iron ingots either side of a gold ingot (the turntable's brass).
  - Broken, either half brings it down, and it drops once.

### Part 3: the Flying Crank Ghost
- **Flying Crank Ghost**: a hooded cheesecloth ghost 1.3 m long, hanging in the air on three steel wires from a crank on a black iron post 2 m tall. Two blocks tall.
  - The post stands on a round iron base plate 50 cm across, a riveted brass gear housing at its foot where its drive comes in. An iron boom reaches forward from the post's top, braced at 45 degrees, to an iron crossbar 94 cm long with a brass pulley hung at each end, and a riveted brass gearbox where they meet. The crank is a brass disc 19 cm across on the gearbox's front, with an iron pin.
  - One wire runs from the crank's pin down to the top of the ghost's hood. The other two run from the pin out over each pulley and down to each of the ghost's wrists.
  - The ghost is a pale face, hollow-eyed and open-mouthed, in a cheesecloth hood; a cape of cheesecloth over its shoulders, its hem torn; and a long robe under it, worn through and torn at its foot into strips of every length. Its arms are thin sleeves with torn cuffs.
  - **It runs on 4 KE a tick**, as the skeleton does, from a shaft, gearbox or kinetic source touching its lower block (a hand crank behind it, against its gear housing, is the way it is drawn to be driven).
  - **While it runs**:
    - the crank turns once round every two seconds. The ghost hangs from its pin, so it rides a circle 12.5 cm across, rising and falling and swinging side to side, and sways 6 degrees against the swing about the top of its hood;
    - as the pin swings away from a pulley it draws that wire in: each arm rises from 22.5 degrees out to 67.5 and falls back, the left as the right falls, and the other way round;
    - its eyes glow pale green, a lamp in its hood, drawn at full light;
    - every ten seconds it moans, the ghast's moan, a little deep, the first as it starts.
  - **Its power stopped**, it keeps going for half a second, then the crank runs down and stops where it is, the ghost and its arms with it, and its eyes go dark.
  - Recipe: string either side of a clock (the wires and the clockwork), over white wool either side of an iron ingot (the cheesecloth and the crossbar), over an iron ingot (the post).
  - Broken, either half brings it down, and it drops once.

### Part 4: the Clockwork Raven
- **Clockwork Raven**: a raven of black enamelled steel plates, life size (65 cm from beak to tail), with a gunmetal beak hooked at its tip and red glass eyes, perched on a brass bird stand 1.3 m tall. Two blocks tall.
  - The stand is a round black iron plinth 50 cm across. On it sits the clockwork's riveted brass drum, 37 cm across, with a brass flywheel on its front. A turned brass column rises to a mahogany perch, an iron collar half way up.
  - The raven grips the perch with iron feet. Its body leans 22.5 degrees, breast up, its wings folded down its sides and its tail angled down behind.
  - **It runs on 2 KE a tick**, a light bird, from a shaft, gearbox or kinetic source touching its lower block.
  - **While it runs**:
    - the drum's flywheel turns once a second;
    - its head looks slowly from side to side, 45 degrees either way, once every six seconds;
    - every six seconds it caws three times, 0.6 seconds apart, a parrot's call pitched low. For each caw its beak opens and its wings beat once, at the top of the beat on the caw;
    - its eyes glow red.
  - **Its power stopped**, the flywheel runs down and stops where it is, its head stops, it folds its wings and shuts its beak, and its eyes go dark.
  - Recipe: two feathers either side of black dye (its enamel), over iron ingots either side of a clock, over a gold ingot (the stand's brass).
  - Broken, either half brings it down, and it drops once.

### Part 5: the Pumpkin-Headed Drummer
- **Pumpkin-Headed Drummer**: a bandsman 1.7 m tall to the top of his head, which is a carved jack-o'-lantern 31 cm across under a black shako. Two blocks tall.
  - He wears a red coat frogged with gold across the chest, gold epaulettes and collar, navy trousers and black boots. He stands on a round black iron plinth with a riveted brass gear housing behind.
  - His bass drum, 50 cm across and 31 cm deep, is slung in front of him on white straps, its heads to either side. Its shell is red lacquer between brass hoops, a white rope zigzagging across.
  - His shako has a peak, a gold band, a brass badge and a red plume. His forearms reach forward to white gloves holding wooden mallets with white felt heads, hanging beside the drumheads.
  - **He runs on 4 KE a tick**, from a shaft, gearbox or kinetic source touching his lower block.
  - **While he runs**:
    - he beats a march two bars long, four beats a bar and a bar every two seconds: the right mallet on the first and third beats of each bar, the left on the second and fourth of the second bar;
    - each stroke is a backswing, 30 degrees out from the drumhead and in again over 0.4 seconds, landing on its beat. The drum sounds with each, lower under the right mallet than the left;
    - his head dips on the first and third beats of each bar;
    - the candle in his head lights his carved eyes and grin.
  - **His power stopped**, his mallets and head come to rest and his face goes dark. Started again, the march begins from the top.
  - Recipe: a carved pumpkin over red wool either side of a note block (his drum), over sticks either side of a clock.
  - Broken, either half brings him down, and he drops once.

### Part 6: the Show Controller
- **Animatronic Show Controller**: the dieselpunk tier, a riveted gunmetal steel control cabinet 87 cm wide, 1 m tall and 50 cm deep, on the JE network. One block.
  - Its front has two cream-faced gauges in chrome bezels and a window on its brass programme drum, its steel pins set in rising rows. Below them sit six amber lamps and a cream enamel nameplate lettered in red.
  - Its sides are louvred, a red beacon sits on its top, a knife switch with a Bakelite knob is on its east side, and a brass cable gland is at its back. Cables connect to it.
  - **It runs on JE**: it holds 8,000 JE and takes up to 128 JE a tick from cables.
  - **It drives every clockwork figure within 8 blocks**, up to 12, without shafts, all in time:
    - switched on (use it to throw its switch) and holding enough JE for them, it cues a show;
    - it holds its figures still for 1.5 seconds, then drives them all from one count, so the organ's waltz, the drummer's march, the werewolf's howls, the raven's caws and the ghost's flight keep time together;
    - it pays for each figure's need at the copper-wound electric motor's rate: 100 JE for 75 KE, rounded up. Five figures (22 KE a tick) cost 30 JE a tick.
  - It looks for figures once a second. When the figures it finds change (one placed, one broken), it cues the show again from the top for all of them.
  - A figure another running controller drives is left to that one.
  - **While its show runs**: the programme drum turns, the show gauge's needle swings, a lamp is lit for each figure cued (up to six; the cast gauge shows the same), the beacon is lit and it gives light 4.
  - **Its show ends** when it is switched off or runs short of JE. Its figures then run down as they would on their own clockwork, and a figure still on a shaft keeps going on that.
  - Recipe: steel ingots round a glass pane, steel gears either side of a clock and redstone.
  - It drops itself (a pickaxe).

## Connections
- Existing input producer: kinetic power, from the hand crank, the steam engine and the electric motor (which takes JE), through iron shafts, brass gearboxes and belts ([kinetic-power.md](kinetic-power.md)). The Show Controller takes JE from cables ([machines-and-power.md](machines-and-power.md)).
- Recipe inputs: bone blocks, a note block, black wool, planks and a clock (the skeleton); bones, brown wool, iron, gold and a clock (the werewolf); string, white wool, iron and a clock (the ghost); feathers, black dye, iron, gold and a clock (the raven); a carved pumpkin, red wool, a note block, sticks and a clock (the drummer); steel ingots, a glass pane, steel gears, a clock and redstone (the controller).
- Existing output consumer: none; a figure is a power sink, like a machine.
- Technology/magic connections (or infrastructure/cosmetic applicability): technology. It is decoration that runs on the kinetic network, a use for a hand crank or a small steam engine before electricity.
- Reachable entry path: a hand crank (planks and an iron shaft), from the first iron. The figure's recipe needs a clock (gold and redstone).
- Which connections are required vs optional; trade and solo routes: all optional, made solo, tradeable.
- How this specialty stays useful without mastering every other branch: a figure stands as decoration unpowered; one hand crank runs it for twenty seconds at a time.

## Balance and automation
- **Units**: KE per tick, as the kinetic network's.
- The Organ-Grinder Skeleton takes 4 KE a tick while it runs, and only on all 4. A hand crank (16 KE a tick) runs four of them; a steam engine (64) sixteen.
- The Animatronic Werewolf takes 8 KE a tick, a heavier figure on a turntable: a hand crank runs two, a steam engine eight.
- The Flying Crank Ghost takes 4 KE a tick, a light ghost on a crank, as the skeleton does.
- The Clockwork Raven takes 2 KE a tick, the lightest: a hand crank runs eight.
- The Pumpkin-Headed Drummer takes 4 KE a tick, as the skeleton does.
- The Show Controller pays for its figures' needs in JE at the copper-wound electric motor's rate, 100 JE for 75 KE, rounded up: never less than the motor and a shaft would cost. It uses nothing while its show is off or cueing, and buffers 8,000 JE.
- The energy goes into the clockwork: nothing comes back out, and it stores nothing. No loop.
- Its recipe gives back only itself.

## Multiplayer and persistence
- **The server runs it**: its block entity, in its lower block, takes power only from the kinetic network's push, counts what it took each tick, and sets its block's `running` (on both halves) only when that changes. It plays the waltz from the server, as sounds every nearby player hears, from the top each time it starts.
- **The client draws it**: from `running` and its own clock. Each client keeps its own count of the tune from when it saw the figure start, so the crank, the arm, the nod and the jaw move in time with what it hears.
- It saves nothing: whether it runs comes again from its power on the first tick after a reload.
- It can't be pushed by a piston.
- IDs (stable from release): the blocks and items `organ_grinder_skeleton`, `animatronic_werewolf`, `flying_crank_ghost`, `clockwork_raven` and `pumpkin_drummer` (`facing`, `half`, `running`), and their block entities of the same names; the block and item `show_controller` (`facing`, `enabled`, `running`, `cast` 0 to 6) and its block entity.
- Every figure shares one clockwork (`ClockworkFigureBlockEntity`): what differs is its need and what it does as it runs, its tune, its howl, its moan, its caws or its march.
- The ghost's moan is the server's, a sound every nearby player hears. How it flies and whether its eyes are lit are drawn by each client from `running` alone.
- **The Show Controller is the server's**:
  - it finds its figures in the set of figures each server level keeps as they tick, so it never searches blocks;
  - it looks once a second, at most 12 figures within 8 blocks;
  - it drives each from one count.
- **Its cue holds its figures still for 1.5 seconds**, longer than any takes to run down on a client (one second at most). So every client sees them stop and counts them from the top together, and the figures' sounds and motions keep time across the show.
- Its switch is thrown by using it, on the server. It saves only its JE: after a reload it cues its show again.
- Its recipe follows the agriculture switch. The block and item stay registered when it is off. It runs on the machines feature's kinetic power: with that off, no hand crank or shaft exists to drive it, and it stands still.

## Dependencies and assets

- Models, blockstates, loot tables, quads and textures are generated by `tools/clockwork_data.py` from `tools/clockwork.py`, through `tools/generate_material_data.py` and `tools/generate_textures.py`.

## Rollout and open questions
- The six parts, each stacked on the last: the Organ-Grinder Skeleton, the Animatronic Werewolf, the Flying Crank Ghost, the Clockwork Raven, the Pumpkin-Headed Drummer and the Show Controller.
- Part 6 added to the shared clockwork a set of the figures on each level, kept as they tick; a hold, for a controller's cue; and a cue, which drives a figure from a controller's count. A figure on its own shafts works as before.
- The Clockwork Raven is not the Raven on a Perch (`raven_perch`, the Mad Scientist's set): that one is a lifelike raven that watches players and ruffles its feathers. This one is a clockwork automaton on the kinetic network.
- Part 2 moved the skeleton's clockwork into the shared `ClockworkFigureBlockEntity` (unchanged in what it does), and its renderer now counts its tune at the server's rate, easing only its crank. The renderer's tune count had been eased too, so the jaw ran a quarter-second behind the notes.
- Open: whether a figure or the controller should also take a redstone signal to start and stop, and whether the controller should let a builder set each figure's start in a sequence.
