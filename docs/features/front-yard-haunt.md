# Front-Yard Haunt Scenes

Status: all five parts implemented: 1 (the path: the Haunted Mailbox and the Pumpkin Path Light), 2 (the bones and the risen: the Skeleton Dog, Cat and Crow and the Rising Zombie), 3 (the doorway: the Gargoyle Door Knocker, the Bat Wreath and the Beware Doormat), 4 (the Crashed Witch) and 5 (the Trick-or-Treat Candy Chute). CI builds them and runs their game tests and client screenshots; not yet played by hand.
Proposal issue: none. The owner asked for it directly on 28 October 2026, as set 7 of the ten sets of fall and Halloween decor ("Then I want you to do 1-10 in parts in that order one by one"), drawn by their rules.
Owner: @jimbozoomer-byte
Target milestone and tier: Discovery tier; the mailbox is made from iron nuggets, a spider eye, red dye and a wooden fence, two path lights from iron bars and a jack-o'-lantern, the skeleton animals from bones and string (the crow with a feather), the Rising Zombie from rotten flesh, a bone and coarse dirt, the knocker from an iron ingot and nuggets, the bat wreath from sticks, paper and black dye, the doormat from wheat and black dye, the Crashed Witch from black and orange wool, leather, a stick and wheat, and the Candy Chute from paper, orange and black dye and a stick.
Primary specialty and supported player role: building. A builder dresses the front of a house for Halloween night, from the street to the door. Nothing in it is needed to progress.

## Player experience
The front yard after dark: what a trick-or-treater passes on the way to the door.

### Part 1: the path
- **Haunted Mailbox** (one block across, two up): a rural mailbox of the large size, 31 cm wide, 34 cm high and 62 cm long, at real height on its post.
  - It is black sheet steel under an arched top, with an orange reflector strip down each flank, on a weathered wooden post with a brace up to its arm. A cobweb is strung between the post and the arm.
  - **An empty hand raises its red flag**, or lowers it.
  - **It is haunted**: now and then its door rattles open a crack and shuts, each mailbox in its own time. **Walk within 4 blocks and its door falls open** and two glowing eyes stare out of the dark inside, blinking now and then. Walk away and it shuts.
  - The haunting is your own sight of it, drawn by your game; it holds nothing.
  - Recipe: an iron nugget, a spider eye and an iron nugget, over an iron nugget, red dye and an iron nugget, over a wooden fence.
  - Broken, it comes away whole and drops itself once.
- **Pumpkin Path Light** (one block across, two up): a shepherd's hook of black wrought iron 1.3 m high, with a foot step to tread it into the ground and its crook bending out over the path.
  - From the crook's tip a little jack-o'-lantern, 25 cm across, hangs on a chain and a wire bail.
  - **Placed lit**: its carved face glows, steady, and its lower block gives light 10. An empty hand puts it out or lights it again.
  - **It sways on its chain**, to and fro along the path and a little from side to side, each hook in its own time.
  - Recipe (makes two): two iron bars, over an iron bar and a jack-o'-lantern, over an iron bar.
  - Broken, it comes away whole and drops itself once.

### Part 2: the bones and the risen
- **Skeleton Dog** (one block): the bleached bones of a big dog sitting up, 69 cm to the top of its skull, at real size.
  - Its front legs stand straight under its chest and its hind legs fold flat beside it. Its ribcage hangs from a spine tipped up 45 degrees from its hips, a shoulder blade on each side.
  - Its neck rises to a skull with two square sockets either side of its muzzle, its jaw agape. Its tail lies along the ground behind it.
  - **Its tail wags**: walk within 6 blocks and it sweeps the ground, 25 degrees either way, growing over its first wag; walk away and it dies away and lies still.
  - Recipe: five bones and a string, shapeless.
- **Skeleton Cat** (one block): a cat's bones arched like a Halloween cat's, stiff-legged, its back bowed up, 55 cm to its tail's tip.
  - Its skull's sockets open wide and square between its brow, the bridge of its nose and its cheekbones. It hisses, its jaw open and its two fangs bared.
  - **Its tail sways** from side to side, 15 degrees either way, its tip curled over, each cat in its own time.
  - Recipe: three bones and a string, shapeless.
- **Skeleton Crow** (one block): a crow's bones perched, its body tipped up, its keel under its ribs, its wings folded along its sides, on two legs and four toes each.
  - Its skull carries its eye sockets on its sides, as a bird's does, and its beak.
  - **It watches you**: within 8 blocks its skull turns toward you, as far as 70 degrees either way, in a bird's quick snaps, every 12 ticks. With no one near it glances about, now this way, now that.
  - Recipe: two bones, a feather and a string, shapeless.
- Each animal faces whoever places it. **An empty hand rattles its bones.** Broken, each drops itself.
- **Rising Zombie** (one block across, two up): a zombie clawing its way out of a mound of fresh-dug earth, out to its waist, at a man's size.
  - The mound is heaped in three layers with clods thrown round it. Its torso is in a torn linen burial shirt, its ribs showing through the tear, and its head lolls to one side.
  - Its two arms are raised from its shoulders and lean out, the right 22.5 degrees and the left 45, so the right reaches higher. Each ends in a hand of four clawed fingers and a thumb.
  - **Its arms claw at the air**, to and fro 12 degrees about their shoulders, out of step, once in 70 ticks.
  - **Come near and they reach higher**: within 5 blocks, they lean as much as 20 degrees nearer upright, the more the nearer you are.
  - **An empty hand makes it groan.**
  - Recipe: rotten flesh round a bone over three coarse dirt.
  - Broken, it comes away whole and drops itself once.
- The wag, the sway, the watching and the clawing are each player's own sight of them, drawn by their game; nothing is held.

### Part 3: the doorway
- **Gargoyle Door Knocker**: a bronze gargoyle's head, 30 cm high on its back plate.
  - It is horned, with deep sockets under its brow and a wrinkled snout. Its jaws hold a ring, with a stud below for the ring to strike.
  - **It hangs where a wreath does**: on the side of a block, or on either face of a wooden door, where it swings with the door.
  - **An empty hand knocks**: it lifts the ring and raps three times, 6 ticks apart. The ring swings out 40 degrees each time and strikes the stud, and the gargoyle's eyes glow ember-red while it raps.
  - On a door, its knock doesn't open the door; sneak to leave it be, or use the door itself.
  - **With a Candy Bag it knocks for trick-or-treat**: at the door it hangs on, or at a wooden door beside the wall it hangs on. It follows the same rules as knocking with the bag.
  - **It is haunted**: now and then it raps by itself, about every few minutes on a wall (on its random ticks) or on a door.
  - Recipe: an iron nugget, an iron ingot and an iron nugget, over two iron nuggets, over an iron nugget.
- **Bat Wreath**: black twigs wound into a ring 75 cm across, two rings of bundles crossing and twigs sticking out of them.
  - Three black paper bats fly off it to the upper left and the right, their wingtips raised. A purple satin bow is tied at its foot.
  - It hangs on the side of a block or on either face of a door, as the autumn and holly wreaths do. It is dressed for good and takes no mums.
  - Recipe: a stick, paper and a stick, over two sticks, over a stick, black dye and a stick.
- **Beware Doormat**: a coir doormat 75 cm by 50 cm, a black border round a skull and crossbones.
  - It lies facing whoever places it, its device the right way up to them, and goes if what is under it does.
  - **Tread on it** and now and then (one step in 8) a heart beats under the boards.
  - Recipe: a wheat, black dye and a wheat, over three wheat.
- Broken, each drops itself; on a door, the knocker and the wreath drop as the other wreaths do.

### Part 4: the Crashed Witch
- **Crashed Witch**: a witch who flew her broom into a tree or a wall and is stuck out to her hips in it.
  - Her ragged black skirt is bunched round the hole she went in by, flaring out to the wall in folds, with a purple petticoat under it.
  - Her legs stick out of it in orange-and-black striped stockings and pointed black boots with gold buckles, splayed a little apart.
  - Her snapped broom's bristles stick out by her lower right, and its handle's splintered other half by her upper left.
  - **She hangs on the side of a solid block or a log**, as the wreaths do, and goes if it does.
  - **Her legs kick**, out of step, 25 degrees up and down at the hip: in a burst now and then, each witch in her own time. While anyone is within 6 blocks, she kicks the whole time, struggling.
  - **An empty hand makes her cackle**, and she kicks harder, for everyone; she cackles again only once she has done.
  - Recipe: black wool, orange wool and black wool, twice over, over leather, a stick and wheat.
  - Broken, she drops herself.

### Part 5: the Trick-or-Treat Candy Chute
- **Trick-or-Treat Candy Chute** (one block across, two up and three long): an open chute 2.5 m long, for handing out candy at a distance.
  - It is a paper chute, a floor and two low sides, wrapped slantwise in orange and black bands.
  - It slopes down 22.5 degrees from a funnel at porch-rail height to a mouth at a child's hand height, about half a metre up.
  - The funnel is open, stepped out in two rings, clamped to a wooden post with a cross foot. A little trestle holds the chute up near its mouth, which has a black lip round it.
  - **Its mouth faces whoever places it** (stand on the path, facing the house), and its funnel is behind, by the porch.
  - **A treat used on it goes in at the funnel**: anything a Candy Bag takes (the item tag `jugcraft:candy_bag_treats`: candy, cookies, soul cakes), one at a time, no faster than one in 4 ticks, and no more than 8 in the chute at once.
  - **It slides down the chute**, quickening, for 20 ticks (a second), tumbling over as it goes.
  - **At the mouth it drops into a Candy Bag**: that of whoever stands within 1.5 blocks of the mouth carrying one, in either hand or anywhere in their inventory. With no one there, or the bag full, it drops out onto the path.
  - An empty hand does nothing to it; it has no light.
  - Recipe: paper, rolled diagonally up the grid, with orange dye beside the middle, a stick and black dye beside the foot.
  - Broken at any block, it comes away whole and drops once, with whatever was still sliding down it.

## How it is drawn (docs/ART_DIRECTION.md, docs/NATURAL_TEXTURES.md)
- Built from boxes at real size, one texel to a model pixel (`tools/front_yard_data.py`), in the Haunted Midway's manner, whose painters it shares. Each two-high piece is cut into its two blocks' models.
- **Sheet steel**: black, lit along its top row and shaded along its foot, with a row of rivets along its seam. An orange reflector strip runs along each flank, with an amber reflector at its front end. Its inside is dark.
- **Weathered wood**: grey-brown stripes along the grain, for the post, arm and brace.
- **Wrought iron**: black, in stripes along it. The crook bends through a bevel at each corner.
- **The jack-o'-lantern**: the midway's ribbed pumpkin, bulging at its middle. Its carved face glows from inside as its own full-bright model, cut out to its holes and steady.
- **The cobweb**: thin pale strands fanning from the corner between the post and the arm, with two strands across them, cut out. Each side fans from that corner as it is seen.
- **Moving parts move**, from each blockstate's `runner` models:
  - `HauntedMailboxRenderer` swings the mailbox's door about its hinge at its foot and draws its eyes full-bright while it is well open;
  - `PumpkinPathLightRenderer` sways the lantern about the crook's tip and draws its glow full-bright while it is lit.
- **The first previews**, fixed before commit:
  - three claw scratches down the mailbox's flank stepped into a grille that read as teeth; the flank now has a reflector strip;
  - the crook was a square bend; it now bends through a bevel at each corner;
  - the little pumpkin was a box; it now bulges at its middle.
- **Bone** (part 2): the midway's bone colour, lit along each bone's upper edge and shaded along the other, its two knuckle ends a tone lighter. A spine or a tail has a knob every other texel along it, the joints between them shaded.
- **The skulls**: square dark sockets and a dark nasal hole, teeth a tone paler. The dog's sockets are painted either side of its muzzle; the cat's open between its brow, nose and cheekbones and are dark inside; the crow's are on the sides of its skull.
- **The rib hoops**: two sides and a bar across their foot round the spine, open above for it.
- **Dug earth**: dark brown, a lit clod with its shadow under it in a staggered pattern.
- **The burial shirt**: coarse linen with a fold every third texel. A tear runs down its front from the left shoulder, green flesh and ribs by turns showing through it, its edges frayed pale. The sleeves are torn off ragged at the elbow.
- **The zombie**: grey-green flesh lit along its left, its fingers' tips black broken nails. Its face is dark hair, two sunken black eyes, a rotted nose and a gaping mouth, three texels by four.
- Moving parts, from each blockstate's `runner` models:
  - `SkeletonAnimalRenderer` sweeps the dog's tail and turns the crow's skull about the upright, and sways the cat's tail from side to side, each about its pivot;
  - `RisingZombieRenderer` claws each arm forward and back about its shoulder and leans it nearer upright as the player nears.
- **Cast bronze** (part 3): bronze lit along each piece's top, a darker right edge, and verdigris gathered along its foot where the rain runs off. Its face has deep black sockets, dark nostrils and polished fangs.
- **The knocker's ring**: an octagon of polished bronze bars, the corners turned 45 degrees.
- **The bat wreath's twigs**: dark grey-brown bark in stripes along each bundle. Each ring of eight bundles is drawn at a texel a pixel, turned in steps of 22.5 or 45 degrees, the only turns a block model takes.
- **The paper bats**: furred bodies and ears. Their wings are cut out, each three texels high: the tip raised at the wrist, the leading edge across, the fingers hanging below with the membrane scalloped between them.
- **The doormat's coir**: bristles woven in rows, two tones by turns, a lit tuft every fourth texel. Its device is black, the bristles round it clipped pale so it reads, drawn the right way up for a visitor coming from the street.
- Moving parts, from the knocker's blockstate `runner` models: `GargoyleKnockerRenderer` swings the ring out about its pivot in the jaws after each knock and lights the eyes full-bright, on a wall from its block entity's mark and on a door (through `DoorWreathRenderer`) from the door attachment's knock.
- **The witch** (part 4): her stockings striped a texel at a time across the leg, orange and black, each stripe lit along its top. Her boots are black with a gold buckle. Her skirt is black gathered in folds, every third texel a tone deeper and every fifth catching a dull violet sheen, its hem cut out into tatters. The broom is brown wood in stripes along it, with straw bristles and a pale splintered end where it snapped.
- `CrashedWitchRenderer` splays each leg out to its side about her hip and kicks it up and down there, from her blockstate's `runner` models.
- **The chute's wrapping** (part 5): orange and black in bands three texels wide, running slantwise round it as a paper strip is wound on, each band lit along its top row. The funnel is wrapped the same; its floor and the mouth's lip are black. The post, foot and trestle are wood in stripes along the grain.
- The chute is three lengths along one line, each turned down 22.5 degrees, the steepest a block model's turn allows short of 45.
- **What slides down it moves**: `CandyChuteRenderer` draws each treat from its block entity's list, from the funnel to the mouth along the chute's floor, faster as it goes, and turns it about the upright as it slides, so a flat item never shows only its edge. The treats are drawn larger than life (0.4 of an item on the ground), as items are, so they read.

- **The part 4 previews**, fixed before commit: her skirt was one flat black panel behind her legs; it now flares out to the wall in three layers, folded, its hem torn.

## Connections
- Existing input producer: vanilla iron, spider eyes, dye, fences and jack-o'-lanterns; bones, string and feathers (skeletons, spiders and chickens); rotten flesh (zombies) and coarse dirt; sticks, paper, wheat, wool and leather.
- Existing system it joins: **trick-or-treating** (the Candy Bag's knock on a wooden door), which the knocker knocks with and whose Candy Bag the chute fills, and the **door wreaths**, which the knocker and the bat wreath hang on doors with.
- The chute takes what a Candy Bag takes (`jugcraft:candy_bag_treats`) and fills it with `CandyBagItem.fill`, as a treat put in by hand would be.
- Existing output consumer: none; they are a yard's decoration.
- Technology/magic connections (or infrastructure/cosmetic applicability): cosmetic.
- Reachable entry path: from the first days, with a little iron, or the drops of the first night's mobs.
- Which connections are required vs optional; trade and solo routes: all optional, crafted solo, and tradeable like any item.
- How this specialty stays useful without mastering every other branch: it is a house's Halloween dressing for anyone's build.

## Balance and automation
- The mailbox costs four iron nuggets, a spider eye, red dye and a fence; two path lights cost four iron bars and a jack-o'-lantern. Each gives back only itself.
- The dog costs five bones and a string, the cat three bones and a string, the crow two bones, a feather and a string, and the Rising Zombie five rotten flesh, a bone and three coarse dirt. Each gives back only itself.
- The knocker costs an iron ingot and five nuggets, the bat wreath five sticks, paper and black dye, and the doormat five wheat and black dye. Each gives back only itself.
- The knocker's trick-or-treat knock is the Candy Bag's own: the same cooldown, the same answer and treats, nothing added.
- The Crashed Witch costs four black wool, two orange wool, leather, a stick and wheat, and gives back only herself.
- The Candy Chute costs three paper, orange dye, black dye and a stick, and gives back only itself. A treat goes down it unchanged: one in, the same one out. It doesn't count as a house visited on the Candy Bag's night, so it adds nothing to trick-or-treating's own rewards.
- They make nothing and take nothing. No loop.

## Multiplayer and persistence
- **The mailbox's flag is the server's**: its blocks' state (`flag`, on both blocks), changed only by a player using it, and saved with the world.
- **Its haunting is each player's own**: their client works out from their own distance and the game's clock when its door rattles and when it falls open. Its block entity (`haunted_mailbox`) keeps on each client whether that player is near and since when; nothing is sent or saved, and nothing ticks on the server.
- **The path light's light is the server's**: `lit`, on both blocks. Its sway is worked out from the game's clock on each client; nothing is sent. Its block entity (`pumpkin_path_light`) keeps nothing; it is there for its renderer.
- **The skeleton animals and the Rising Zombie hold nothing**: their moving parts are worked out on each client from its own distance and the game's clock. The animals' shared block entity (`skeleton_animal`) keeps on each client whether that player is near and since when, and where the crow's head is turning; the zombie's (`rising_zombie`) keeps nothing. Nothing is sent or saved, and nothing ticks on the server.
- The mailbox, the path light and the Rising Zombie can't be pushed by a piston; a piston pops a skeleton animal off, and it drops itself.
- **The knocker's knocks are the server's**: an empty hand or a Candy Bag knocks, and the server raps it (its scheduled ticks on a wall, its door attachment's ticks on a door). Its block entity (`gargoyle_knocker`) marks each knock on every side, from a block event, for its renderer. On a door the attachment keeps a count of knocks, sent to every client so each draws the ring swinging; nothing about a knock is saved. While it raps, another knock waits.
- **Its haunting** is the server's too, so everyone hears it: one random tick in 3 on a wall, one tick in 3,000 on a door, it knocks.
- **Hanging on a door**, the knocker and the bat wreath use the door wreaths' attachment (`door_wreath`), unchanged in what it saves: the block state of what hangs there. That attachment now asks what hangs on it what it is (whether it takes mums, its box on a door, its use, its tick). It no longer singles out the holly wreath by class, and the autumn and holly wreaths behave as before.
- The knocker and the bat wreath pop off when pushed, as the wreaths do; so does the doormat.
- **What slides down the chute is the server's**: its block entity (`candy_chute`, on its first block) keeps each treat and the game time it went in, saves them with the world, and sends the list to every client when it changes, so everyone sees the same candy sliding. The server delivers each treat 20 ticks after it went in. Its tick does nothing while the chute is empty, and a chute holds at most 8.
- A treat goes in only by a player using it on the chute in reach, one item a use, at most one in 4 ticks; the server checks that it is a treat. What comes out is what went in, into a bag or onto the path; nothing is lost if a bag is full. The chute can't be pushed by a piston.
- **The witch's kicking is each player's own sight of her**, from their distance and the game's clock. Her cackle is the server's: an empty hand plays it and sends a block event, and her block entity (`crashed_witch`) marks it on every side so every client draws her kicking harder. Nothing is saved, and nothing ticks on the server. She pops off when pushed.
- IDs (stable from release):
  - the block and item `haunted_mailbox` (`facing`, `part` 0 to 1, `lit` (unused), `flag`, model-only `runner` 0 to 2; block entity `haunted_mailbox`, saving nothing);
  - the block and item `pumpkin_path_light` (`facing`, `part` 0 to 1, `lit`, model-only `runner` 0 to 2; block entity `pumpkin_path_light`, saving nothing).
  - the blocks and items `skeleton_dog`, `skeleton_cat` and `skeleton_crow` (`facing`, model-only `runner` 0 to 1; one block entity `skeleton_animal` for the three, saving nothing);
  - the block and item `rising_zombie` (`facing`, `part` 0 to 1, `lit` (unused), model-only `runner` 0 to 2; block entity `rising_zombie`, saving nothing).
  - the block and item `gargoyle_knocker` (`facing`, `flowers` (unused, as the wreath's kind), model-only `runner` 0 to 2; block entity `gargoyle_knocker`, saving nothing);
  - the block and item `bat_wreath` (`facing`, `flowers` (unused));
  - the block and item `beware_doormat` (`facing`);
  - the block and item `crashed_witch` (`facing`, model-only `runner` 0 to 2; block entity `crashed_witch`, saving nothing);
  - the block and item `candy_chute` (`facing`, `part` 0 to 5, `lit` (unused); block entity `candy_chute`, saving `sliding`: each treat and the game time it went in).
- Their recipes follow the agriculture switch. The blocks and items stay registered when it is off.

## Dependencies and assets
