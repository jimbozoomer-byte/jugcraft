# Moving pirate ships

Owner scope, 10 November 2026 (relayed by Codex): genuinely moving ocean ships with visible crews; bounded patrols in loaded water; boarding encounters; saved ship and crew identities; no forced chunk loading; player claims and settlements preserved; villagers never targeted; no sea raids on towns. The static galleon (`OwnerGalleon`, `supps_galleon`) and every existing `supps_*` ID are unchanged.

This record covers the ship itself. The crew, their stations and boarding are in [pirate-ship-crews.md](pirate-ship-crews.md); the model and textures come from `tools/pirate_ship.py`.

## What exists

- **`jugcraft:pirate_ship`** (`menagerie/PirateShip`): a 6 x 16 brig in the frame of `CrewedVessel` (x to port, y up from the waterline, z to the bow; deck at y 1.0). It implements `CrewedVessel` with the ten standard stations of `PirateCrew`.
- **Walkable deck** (`jugcraft:pirate_ship_deck`, `PirateShipDeck`): an entity's box cannot turn, so the deck is squares along the keel, one block tall from the waterline, so their tops are the deck. Since 14 November there are six, sized by their synced index to follow the hull's taper (`PirateDeckPlan`: sides 5, 5, 5, 4.5, 2.75 and 1.5 at local z −5, −1, 2, 3.375, 5.625 and 6.875): none reaches more than a pixel past the drawn hull's side and none leaves a gap of 0.6 blocks inside the bulwarks. Before, four 5 x 5 squares (z −5.5, −2, 2, 5.5) overreached the fine bow by up to two blocks, so a boarder could stand on air beside the stem. Players, boarders and dropped crew stand on them. They are never saved; the ship makes them again when it loads, and they go when it goes. Hitting or using one hits or uses the ship. At yaws between the right angles a square's corner reaches up to half a block past the hull.
- **Patrol.** Within 64 blocks of the place it was raised (its anchor, saved), waypoint to waypoint (each 16-64 blocks from the anchor, one candidate tried a tick). Top speed 0.12 blocks a tick, turning 1 degree a tick, server-authoritative and eased on clients (`SmoothFlight`). It makes way only while a crew member holds the helm (`PirateCrew.helmManned`), the crew have not hove to, it is afloat, and no player stands on its deck. Every step (the turn and the move together) is checked before it is taken: the box enclosing the hull's old and new footprints, widened by the farthest a corner swings in that turn, must be clear (`PirateWaters.sweep`, the rules below), so the stern cannot swing into a bank, a block or protected water while the way ahead is open. A refused step stops the ship, which chooses another heading; nothing it does loads a chunk. (10 November review: the first push checked only a point 3 blocks ahead every 10 ticks.) Pistons never move the ship or its deck squares (both push as `IGNORE_ENTITY`), so nothing but its own checked step moves it. A ship added without a launch (an operator's `/summon`, with no anchor saved) anchors its patrol where it first floats and saves that anchor; before, it anchored at the world's origin and never sailed. A ship saved before that first tick saves no anchor, so it anchors where it is next placed.
- **Sailing beside protected land (a known limit, by design).** The patrol checks only its own swept volume, so a patrolling ship may sail close beside the edge of a claim, the town or a village, even though none is within 48 blocks of where it was raised. It never enters protected land or water, and shot protection at the point of impact keeps its fire from harming anything inside a protected area.
- **Where it may be** (`PirateWaters.clear`): two volumes, each turned to the yaw and enclosed by an axis-aligned box (a conservative superset), inside the world border and on loaded chunks only. Under the water, the 6 x 16 hull: the waterline block and 2 more must be open water: plain water (still or flowing), a bubble column, or the sea's own plants (kelp and seagrass). Any other waterlogged block (a slab, a fence, a rail) refuses the place, as do ice and every block. Above it, the rig, which reaches past the hull (rails, bowsprit, rudder, stern lantern, sails at their sway: x ±4.0, z −9.5 to +11.75, from the model rounded outward). It is checked from the waterline row up: that row must be open water or air across the whole rig, since the bow's stem rises through the top of the row a quarter block ahead of the hull's 16 blocks. The 13 blocks above it must be air and nothing else, so a torch, rail, flower, vine or fire refuses the place too. (10 November review: the rig's reach beyond the hull was not checked; then the waterline row ahead of the bow, and non-colliding clutter in either volume, were not.) In both volumes there is no protection anywhere in that volume, tested exactly: land claims per chunk (whole columns), operator regions by cuboid intersection (`OperatorRegions.intersects`, added for this: a one-block region or one at bridge height counts), and the town's protected columns at a height inside both the town's protected range and the hull's. Nothing in it loads a chunk or changes the world. (10 November review: the first push sampled protection every 4 blocks at the waterline only.) Codex's local `PirateWaters` draft has the same signatures (`clear`, `footprint`, `loaded`); either may replace the other.
- **Natural ships** (`PirateSeas`, saved with the overworld as `jugcraft:pirate_seas`):
  - every 1,200 ticks, at most 4 players tried, each with a 1 in 4 chance; players in creative or spectator are skipped, and nothing spawns on Peaceful or with `spawn_mobs`/`spawn_monsters` off;
  - the ship is raised 48-96 blocks from the player over ocean (`#is_ocean`) at sea level, only where the water is clear, with no town, claim, operator region or village within 48 blocks over its whole height. Every chunk within those 48 blocks must be loaded, since what cannot be seen counts as settled, so a village in an unloaded chunk is never missed; on a server with a short view distance, ships appear less often. Villages are looked for in every one of those chunks at the waterline and at 32 and 64 blocks above it, each look reaching a section up and down, so a village on the cliffs over the sea counts (`PirateSeas.settlementNear`, which says why a place is refused). (10 November review: unloaded chunks counted as empty, and only the waterline section was searched.)
  - at most 3 ships afloat and crewed, counted from the saved ledger, so an unloaded ship still counts however long it stays unloaded; one ship per 512-block region; a region rests 3 days (72,000 ticks) after its ship is beaten, sunk or removed;
  - only a positive end releases a ship's record (sunk, removed, or beaten and gone); nothing expires for being unseen. A removed ship's id is kept as a tombstone (the newest 1,024), and a natural ship asks the ledger when it loads: a tombstoned copy (an older chunk save, a restored backup) goes quietly with its crew, rolling no loot and spilling nothing. (10 November review: the first push forgot ships unseen for 7 days, which let a reloaded ship duplicate its region and pass the cap.)
  - the crew are seated (`PirateCrew.provision`) before the ship is added, and ship and crew are added together once (`addFreshEntityWithPassengers`).
- **Crew roster and own broadside** (crew branch, merged here): victory is `PirateCrew.defeated`, decided from a saved roster of the crew's deaths and permanent removals, never from absence; when the ship is removed for good it calls `PirateCrew.forget`, and its crew fight on unlinked. A crew member's cannonball never hits its own ship, deck squares or shipmates (`OwnerCannonball.canHitEntity`), but another ship's hull is fair game; a cannonball that strikes a hull deals its damage server-side through the ship's own rules. A real-hull test fires one straight across the deck.
- **Hold and loot.** 27 slots, saved. Empty while the crew hold out. When `PirateCrew.defeated` first answers true for a ship the spawner crewed, the hold is filled once from the static galleon's own chest table (`jugcraft:supps_loot/galleon/chest`, read, not changed), with a seed fixed by the ship's UUID; the ship heaves to and its flag is struck. It is never rolled again: not on reload, not after it is emptied. A ship nobody crewed (summoned by an operator) never rolls. The crew code rolls, gives and drops no hold loot. Use a beaten ship (its deck) to open the hold; a crewed one refuses.
- **Damage and sinking.** Every blow is judged by the shared protection rules at the ship's own place and at the point of impact, so a claim, town or operator region laid over a ship that is already there shields it. Players harm it by hand, with arrows or with cannonballs only where they may build; another pirate ship's crew harm it with cannon fire (sea fights) only where the unprivileged pirate identity (`Authority.pirateCrewActor`, with no claim or town exemption) may change things; its own crew never do (their balls pass over their own deck and shipmates). 300 points sink it; an operator in creative removes it outright, spilling the hold. A sinking ship spills its hold where it lies, ejects its crew (who fight on unlinked), settles for 200 ticks and is gone. It settles 0.03 blocks a tick, but never below the hull's checked depth (its waterline row less 2), and only while the way down, across the rig's box from the keel's new row to the waterline, is open water or air, loaded and unprotected from keel to masthead (`PirateWaters.maySink`). So it never settles into the seabed, a block or an operator region under it. (10 November review: it sank 6 blocks unchecked.) A beaten ship with an empty hold that no player comes within 48 blocks of for 6,000 ticks sinks on its own.
- **Renderer** (`client/PirateShipRenderer`): the hull from `pirate_ship_quads.json` (via `DecorQuads`), turned to the yaw and rocking slowly; sails swaying with headway; the flag fluttering (not drawn once beaten); wheel and rudder turned by the synced helm; a cannon at each gunner station turned to its broadside. Pivots and cannon trunnions come from `pirate_ship_rig.json` (ship-local pixels; culling box in blocks), with defaults from the agreed layout where the file or a key is missing. The rig is read again after every client resource reload (F3+T, a resource-pack change). The quads come through `DecorQuads`, which, like every decoration's, keeps what it read until the game restarts.

## Refinement, 14 November 2026

- **Small crews.** A natural ship carries three to five plunderers (`PirateCrew.crew`, `PirateFairness.crewFor`): the captain at the helm and one gunner on each side (the aft guns), then one or two deck hands forward, by the armour of the player it was raised for (3 under 15 points, 4 under 20, 5 from 20). All four guns are still drawn; the unmanned ones are silent. The ten stations stay defined: a ship saved before small crews keeps its ten.
- **Old ships keep working (save format).** The ship saves `CrewFormat`: 1, a legacy ten-station ship (which a save without the field means), or 2, a small crew. Nothing is migrated in place: an old ship keeps its saved crew at their old stations, its roster with its recorded deaths, its hold and its `LootRolled`, `HeaveTo` and `Sunk` flags exactly; it is beaten, and rolls its hold, by the same roster rule. Loading never reseats, rerolls or resets anything. No ship is ever crewed twice: `PirateCrew.provision` seats nobody on a ship that carries crew or that the roster already holds (provisioned before, its crew since lost or gone). The sea's ledger is format 2 (`version`): it adds the players' rests; a format-1 ledger reads with every ship, rest and tombstone kept.
- **Captain lost.** With nobody living at the helm for 60 ticks, the next of the crew aboard takes the wheel, a deck hand first, then the lookout, then a gunner (lowest station first): it moves to the helm's station; nobody is made and the roster is unchanged. The ship lies still meanwhile. This holds for old ten-crew ships too.
- **Abandoned ships.** A crewed ship that is not beaten, with none of its crew aboard or near (they vanished without dying: Peaceful's despawn, an admin's discard), and no player within 48 blocks for 6,000 ticks, is scuttled: it sinks with an empty hold (never rolled, so no loot) and its removal frees its place in the ledger. Before, such a ship held a place under the cap for ever.
- **Routes.** A waypoint is 16 to 54 blocks from the anchor (inside the 64-block beat by twice the slowest turning circle), taken only at the end of a straight lane of open sea (`PirateWaters.laneClear`, sampled every 2 blocks along it and 6 either side: the waterline block and 3 below it open water, air above) with a turning basin of 13 blocks round it, every chunk under them fully loaded and ticking, and no claim, town or operator region within 16 blocks of lane or basin, keel to masthead. Each step is still swept exactly. A refused step drops the waypoint; 60 refused steps in a row and the ship goes astern, straight, at 0.03 blocks a tick for up to 80 ticks; boxed in astern too, it lies still 100 ticks and tries again (`PirateCourse.Helm`, saved). It is never moved any other way. (A's review item 3: the beat may reach 16 blocks past the raising clearance, but lanes now keep 16 blocks from protection.)
- **Chunks.** "Loaded" now means fully loaded (`getChunkNow`), never a chunk still on its way in (A's review item 7), and every step needs every chunk it touches to be entity-ticking, so a ship never sails into the edge of the loaded world and freezes there with its crew.
- **Collision.** A step whose swept hull (waterline to two blocks over the deck) would meet another pirate ship or its deck squares, a boat, or a player not aboard it is refused (`PirateWaters.unobstructed`). Fish, items, its own crew and squares are not in the way.
- **Fairness.** Ships are raised only near a survival or adventure player wearing at least 10 armour points, at most one a game day near any one player (`PirateSeas` keeps each player's rest), never within 160 blocks of another ship's anchor, still at most three afloat. Their crews engage only a player with 10 or more armour points, or one who has provoked that ship within 5 minutes (struck the ship or one of its crew, or set foot on its deck); anyone else sails past unmolested. With one finite hold a ship, rolled once, that bounds what a player can take to about one hold a day for ships raised near them.
- **Motion on the water** (`PirateMotion`, shared by server and client): the swell's roll (0.5°), pitch (0.2°) and heave (a fifth of a pixel); a heel outward in a turn (0.3° at full rudder and way) and to leeward of the wind (0.25°), at most 0.4°. Small on purpose (Codex's review of checkpoint 2): the walkable deck squares stay level, so the drawn deck may part from them by at most 1.5 pixels (`PirateMotion.DECK_CAP`); the most it does, over every deck square and pose, is 1.25 pixels (`PirateLogicTests`). The visible motion is in the sails (sway with the headway), the yards (braced to the wind), the flag and the helm. There is no sinking list (Codex's local review removed the old 14° list, which carried the hull out of the water and protection it is checked in): a sinking ship only settles. The wind is one of 16 compass points a game day, shifting over the last 600 ticks of the day; the ship syncs the wind off its bow and whether its sails are set (furled when hove to, beaten, sinking or unhelmed).
- **One phase on both sides.** The swell is driven by a phase, not an entity's age: the world's game time plus an offset fixed by the ship's UUID (so saved with it and known to every client), wrapped to a 2,400-tick period in which every swell term completes whole cycles, plus the partial tick on the client (`PirateShip.swellPhase`). The server's crew seats (`PirateCrew.seat`, through `CrewedVessel.lean`) and the client's drawing therefore agree, however late a client began tracking the ship, and after a save and reload. The renderer applies the heave, yaw, roll and pitch in that order, as `lean` does. The walkable deck squares stay level: a player's feet may be up to 1.25 pixels off the drawn deck at the swell's extremes (no position compensation).
- **Clearance envelope.** With the most roll (0.9°), pitch and heave, the drawn hull reaches 1.6653 blocks under the waterline (the rudder and sternpost's foot): inside `PirateWaters.KEEL`, now 1.75 (it was 1.625, the keel at rest; checkpoint 2's larger swell reached 1.7398), and inside the 2 checked rows of water under the waterline row. In the waterline row it reaches 3.0001 blocks from the centreline (inside the rig's checked 4.0); under it 1.3988 (inside the hull's 3.0). `PirateLogicTests` and the art test check this for every pose.
- **Standing on the deck** (`PirateDecks.standingOnDeck(LivingEntity)`, an API for the Naval Cutlass's boarding bonus): true only when an entity's feet rest on a deck square (on the ground, within a pixel of its top) over the ship's walkable deck at its yaw (`PirateDeckPlan`, exact at any yaw), or for the ship's own crew riding at a deck-level station. False beside the hull, on a shore or dock, on a block or the rigging above the deck, in the main top, in the air, below the deck, or in a boat. One small loaded-entity query round the feet, only when called.
- **The captain steers.** The crew member at the helm is drawn with a hand on the wheel's spoke, rising and falling with the rudder, and the sword hand at his side, unless he is fighting or using his spyglass.

## Saved state

On the ship: `AnchorX/Y/Z` (only once anchored), `HasWaypoint`, `WaypointX/Z`, `Natural`, `Crewed`, `LootRolled`, `HeaveTo`, `Sunk`, `Damage`, `Sinking`, `Idle`, the hold's `Items`, and (14 November) `CrewFormat`, `StallRefused`, `StallBacking`, `StallResting` and `Abandoned`. Provocations and the helm's succession timer are not saved: after a reload a player must provoke the ship again, and a helmless ship waits the full time again. The crew ride as passengers, saved inside the ship by vanilla. In `jugcraft:pirate_seas`: each natural ship's UUID, region, anchor, state (active or defeated) and when its chunk last held it, each resting region's end time, the tombstones of removed ships, and (format 2) each resting player's end time and the `version`.

Fixes from the 10 November review are covered as well:
  - a piston pushing into the ship's box and two deck squares moves nothing (`pistonsNeverMoveTheShipOrItsDeck`);
  - a place beside an unloaded chunk within the clearance raises no ship and loads nothing (`anUnloadedChunkInTheClearanceRaisesNoShip`);
  - a block in the waterline row just ahead of the bow refuses the place and the step (`theBowsStemNeedsTheWaterlineRowClear`);

  - a ship saved before its first tick saves no `AnchorX/Y/Z` and, reloaded, anchors where it is placed on its first tick, while an anchored save keeps its anchor (`anUnanchoredSaveAnchorsWhereItIsReloaded`).
