# Moving pirate ships

Owner scope, 10 November 2026 (relayed by Codex): genuinely moving ocean ships with visible crews; bounded patrols in loaded water; boarding encounters; saved ship and crew identities; no forced chunk loading; player claims and settlements preserved; villagers never targeted; no sea raids on towns. The static galleon (`OwnerGalleon`, `supps_galleon`) and every existing `supps_*` ID are unchanged.

This record covers the ship itself. The crew, their stations and boarding are in [pirate-ship-crews.md](pirate-ship-crews.md); the model and textures come from `tools/pirate_ship.py`.

## Status

Implemented on the review branch `claude/nov10-pirate-ship` (based on the crew branch). It has not been compiled or run here (no Gradle or Minecraft in the cloud environment). The hull art is a separate branch; until it lands, the renderer draws nothing and logs that `pirate_ship_quads.json` is missing.

## What exists

- **`jugcraft:pirate_ship`** (`menagerie/PirateShip`): a 6 x 16 brig in the frame of `CrewedVessel` (x to port, y up from the waterline, z to the bow; deck at y 1.0). It implements `CrewedVessel` with the ten standard stations of `PirateCrew`.
- **Walkable deck** (`jugcraft:pirate_ship_deck`, `PirateShipDeck`): an entity's box cannot turn, so the deck is four 5 x 5 squares along the keel (local z −5.5, −2, 2, 5.5), one block tall from the waterline, so their tops are the deck. Players, boarders and dropped crew stand on them. They are never saved; the ship makes them again when it loads, and they go when it goes. Hitting or using one hits or uses the ship. At yaws between the right angles a square's corner reaches up to half a block past the hull.
- **Patrol.** Within 64 blocks of the place it was raised (its anchor, saved), waypoint to waypoint (each 16-64 blocks from the anchor, one candidate tried a tick). Top speed 0.12 blocks a tick, turning 1 degree a tick, server-authoritative and eased on clients (`SmoothFlight`). It makes way only while a crew member holds the helm (`PirateCrew.helmManned`), the crew have not hove to, it is afloat, and no player stands on its deck. Every step (the turn and the move together) is checked before it is taken: the box enclosing the hull's old and new footprints, widened by the farthest a corner swings in that turn, must be clear (`PirateWaters.sweep`, the rules below), so the stern cannot swing into a bank, a block or protected water while the way ahead is open. A refused step stops the ship, which chooses another heading; nothing it does loads a chunk. (10 November review: the first push checked only a point 3 blocks ahead every 10 ticks.)
- **Where it may be** (`PirateWaters.clear`): two volumes, each turned to the yaw and enclosed by an axis-aligned box (a conservative superset), inside the world border and on loaded chunks only. Under the water, the 6 x 16 hull: the waterline block and 2 more must be water with nothing solid (kelp and seagrass pass). Above it, the rig, which reaches past the hull (rails, bowsprit, rudder, stern lantern, sails at their sway: x ±4.0, z −9.5 to +11.75, from the model rounded outward): 13 blocks clear of anything solid or liquid. (10 November review: the rig's reach beyond the hull was not checked.) In both: no block entity; and no protection anywhere in that volume, tested exactly: land claims per chunk (whole columns), operator regions by cuboid intersection (`OperatorRegions.intersects`, added for this: a one-block region or one at bridge height counts), and the town's protected columns at a height inside both the town's protected range and the hull's. Nothing in it loads a chunk or changes the world. (10 November review: the first push sampled protection every 4 blocks at the waterline only.) Codex's local `PirateWaters` draft has the same signatures (`clear`, `footprint`, `loaded`); either may replace the other.
- **Natural ships** (`PirateSeas`, saved with the overworld as `jugcraft:pirate_seas`):
  - every 1,200 ticks, at most 4 players tried, each with a 1 in 4 chance; players in creative or spectator are skipped, and nothing spawns on Peaceful or with `spawn_mobs`/`spawn_monsters` off;
  - the ship is raised 48-96 blocks from the player over ocean (`#is_ocean`) at sea level, only where the water is clear, with no town, claim, operator region or village within 48 blocks over its whole height (villages read only in loaded chunks);
  - at most 3 ships afloat and crewed, counted from the saved ledger, so an unloaded ship still counts however long it stays unloaded; one ship per 512-block region; a region rests 3 days (72,000 ticks) after its ship is beaten, sunk or removed;
  - only a positive end releases a ship's record (sunk, removed, or beaten and gone); nothing expires for being unseen. A removed ship's id is kept as a tombstone (the newest 1,024), and a natural ship asks the ledger when it loads: a tombstoned copy (an older chunk save, a restored backup) goes quietly with its crew, rolling no loot and spilling nothing. (10 November review: the first push forgot ships unseen for 7 days, which let a reloaded ship duplicate its region and pass the cap.)
  - the crew are seated (`PirateCrew.provision`) before the ship is added, and ship and crew are added together once (`addFreshEntityWithPassengers`).
- **Crew roster and own broadside** (crew branch, merged here): victory is `PirateCrew.defeated`, decided from a saved roster of the crew's deaths and permanent removals, never from absence; when the ship is removed for good it calls `PirateCrew.forget`, and its crew fight on unlinked. A crew member's cannonball never hits its own ship, deck squares or shipmates (`OwnerCannonball.canHitEntity`); a real-hull test fires one straight across the deck.
- **Hold and loot.** 27 slots, saved. Empty while the crew hold out. When `PirateCrew.defeated` first answers true for a ship the spawner crewed, the hold is filled once from the static galleon's own chest table (`jugcraft:supps_loot/galleon/chest`, read, not changed), with a seed fixed by the ship's UUID; the ship heaves to and its flag is struck. It is never rolled again: not on reload, not after it is emptied. A ship nobody crewed (summoned by an operator) never rolls. The crew code rolls, gives and drops no hold loot. Use a beaten ship (its deck) to open the hold; a crewed one refuses.
- **Damage and sinking.** Only players and their shots harm it (300 points); an operator in creative removes it outright, spilling the hold. A sinking ship spills its hold where it lies, ejects its crew (who fight on unlinked), settles for 200 ticks and is gone. A beaten ship with an empty hold that no player comes within 48 blocks of for 6,000 ticks sinks on its own.
- **Renderer** (`client/PirateShipRenderer`): the hull from `pirate_ship_quads.json` (via `DecorQuads`), turned to the yaw and rocking slowly; sails swaying with headway; the flag fluttering (not drawn once beaten); wheel and rudder turned by the synced helm; a cannon at each gunner station turned to its broadside. Pivots and cannon trunnions come from `pirate_ship_rig.json` (ship-local pixels; culling box in blocks), with defaults from the agreed layout where the file or a key is missing. The rig is read again after every client resource reload (F3+T, a resource-pack change). The quads come through `DecorQuads`, which, like every decoration's, keeps what it read until the game restarts.

## Saved state

On the ship: `AnchorX/Y/Z`, `HasWaypoint`, `WaypointX/Z`, `Natural`, `Crewed`, `LootRolled`, `HeaveTo`, `Sunk`, `Damage`, `Sinking`, `Idle` and the hold's `Items`. The crew ride as passengers, saved inside the ship by vanilla. In `jugcraft:pirate_seas`: each natural ship's UUID, region, anchor, state (active or defeated) and when its chunk last held it, each resting region's end time, and the tombstones of removed ships.

## Tests

`PirateShipGameTests` (server; registered): clear water and its refusals, including an unloaded place that stays unloaded; the deck's height and a pig standing on it, the squares following a turn; sailing only when helmed, stopping dead without a helmsman, hove to, or with a player aboard; the hold rolled exactly once (equal to the table's roll for the ship's seed) and never refilled through more ticks and a reload; saving; sinking by players only, with the hold spilt; the spawn ledger's region, cap, rest and save rules, a ship unloaded for weeks still holding its place, and tombstones; a removed ship's stale copy vanishing without loot; operator regions anywhere in the hull's volume (one block between old sample points, bridge height) blocking it while one above the masts does not; the stern not swinging into a block or protected cell just off the quarter while straight on is allowed; the rig needing more room above the water than the hull below it; and the crew's own broadside passing over the real deck while a player's ball is stopped by it. Most of them answer the ship's questions to its crew from the test (`PirateShipTesting`, test code only), so they do not depend on crew behaviour; `realCrewDeathsBeatTheShipAndOpenItsHold` uses no stand-ins: a provisioned crew of ten on the real roster, the ship holding out while one lives, the hold rolled exactly once on the last recorded death, and the roster forgotten when the ship is removed.
